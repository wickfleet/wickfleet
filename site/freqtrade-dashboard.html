A dashboard for all your Freqtrade bots

FreqUI is the dashboard Freqtrade ships with, and it is good at one bot. This page covers how far it goes with several bots, and what Wickfleet adds for someone who runs three or more.

What FreqUI does with several bots

  • You add each bot by its URL and login in the browser, and switch between them
  • Its dashboard has a bot comparison table: one row per bot and a summary row
  • When FreqUI and a bot are served from different addresses, that bot needs FreqUI's address in api_server.CORS_origins, or the browser blocks the calls

With one or two bots that is often all you need.

Where it stops

FreqUI has no server of its own, on purpose. Its maintainer has explained why in the issue tracker (#1094). For a fleet that leaves three gaps:

  • The bot list and every bot login live in one browser. A second device starts empty
  • The summary row adds up profit, and balance when the selected bots share one stake currency, and marks a sum that mixes dry and live. There is no total per stake currency, and no view that keeps dry and live apart
  • A bot has one login. Showing the bots to someone means sharing it

What the Wickfleet dashboard shows

ViewShows
Fleetper bot: invested, value, profit after fees, fees, return, trades and fee per trade. Totals per stake currency: invested, value, profit after fees, fees and return. Dry and live are never summed together
Compareseveral bots replayed over the same window: profit, drawdown, ratios and a robustness verdict per bot
Riskopen trades, capital at stake, exposure per pair and per coin, drawdown, stoploss distance and pair overlap across bots
Each botthe FreqUI trade view, unchanged
Metricsper bot as Prometheus gauges at the hub's /metrics: up, investment, value, net profit, fees, trades and fee per trade, for your own Grafana

One login, one address

  • The bot list lives on the server, so every browser and device sees the same fleet
  • People log in once and get a role: viewer, operator or admin. On Compare and Risk a viewer sees percentages and verdicts, no balances. More in roles and login
  • Bot API keys stay on the server. The hub adds each bot's key per request
  • Every bot is served under the same address at /bots/<name>/, so there is no CORS_origins to maintain

The cost is one more service to run. Without a need for any of this, plain FreqUI is simpler. The longer comparison is in the FAQ.

Try it

git clone https://github.com/wickfleet/wickfleet.git
cd wickfleet
make quickstart

Needs Docker with Compose v2, git and make, on macOS or Linux. Everything runs on one machine, bound to 127.0.0.1, and a dry-run demo bot is waiting in the fleet. More in the install guide, or look first at the read-only demo.
